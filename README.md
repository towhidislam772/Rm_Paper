# Evaluating AI Governance and Regulation in Developing Countries

> A Comparative Analysis with a Proposed Framework  
> Submitted to **ICCA 2026** — 4th International Conference on Computing Advancements  
> Dhaka, Bangladesh · October 15–16, 2026

---

## Overview

This paper addresses a critical gap in the AI governance literature: the absence of a practical, developing-country-specific framework for evaluating governance readiness. It examines four countries — **Bangladesh, India, Nigeria, and Brazil** — across five governance dimensions, and proposes the **Developing Country AI Governance (DCAG) Framework** alongside a **Governance Readiness Dashboard** prototype.

---

## Research Questions

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| RQ1 | How do Bangladesh, India, Nigeria, and Brazil compare in AI governance readiness across policy, institutional capacity, risk management, technical infrastructure, and ethical inclusion? |
| RQ2 | What dimensions and indicators should a practical, context-sensitive AI governance evaluation framework for developing countries include? |

---

## Methodology

- **Design:** Qualitative comparative research
- **Method:** Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following PRISMA guidelines + national policy document content analysis
- **Databases:** Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar
- **Corpus:** 29 primary sources (timeframe: 2019–2026)
- **Validation instruments:** EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, UNESCO Ethics Recommendation, OECD AI Principles, UN High-Level Advisory Body Final Report
- **Scoring rubric:** 3-point scale — Low (1) / Medium (2) / High (3)

---

## The DCAG Framework

Five layered dimensions, ordered by dependency (D1 is foundational; D5 requires all lower layers):

| Dimension | Key Indicators |
|-----------|---------------|
| **D1 — Policy & Legal Framework** | AI strategy, legal liability, risk classification, enforcement mechanism |
| **D2 — Institutional Capacity** | Dedicated AI regulator, inter-agency coordination, skilled workforce, budget |
| **D3 — Risk Management** | Risk assessment, audit, incident reporting, sectoral standards |
| **D4 — Technical Infrastructure** | Data ecosystems, compute capacity, internet penetration, interoperability |
| **D5 — Ethics & Inclusion** | Bias mitigation, public participation, minority protection, transparency |

## Key Results

### Comparative Governance Readiness Scores (out of 15)

| Dimension | Bangladesh | India | Nigeria | Brazil |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| D1: Policy & Legal | Medium (2) | **High (3)** | Medium (2) | **High (3)** |
| D2: Institutional | Low (1) | Medium (2) | Low (1) | Medium (2) |
| D3: Risk Mgmt. | Low (1) | Medium (2) | Low (1) | Medium (2) |
| D4: Technical | Low (1) | **High (3)** | Low (1) | **High (3)** |
| D5: Ethics | Medium (2) | Medium (2) | Medium (2) | Medium (2) |
| **Total** | **7/15** | **12/15** | **7/15** | **12/15** |

### Key Finding
Policy ambition significantly outstrips institutional and enforcement capacity in all four countries — by up to 38 percentage points (Bangladesh: 85% ambition vs. 47% readiness).

## The Governance Readiness Dashboard

A Progressive Web Application (PWA) prototype with offline support for low-bandwidth environments. Five functional screens:

1. **Country Overview** — Radar chart for at-a-glance policymaker decisions
2. **Dimension Deep Dive** — Indicator-level evidence and citations for researchers
3. **Comparative Analysis View** — Multi-country comparison for international organizations
4. **Recommendation Engine** — Actionable guidance for government ministries
5. **Policy Tracker** — Longitudinal readiness monitoring

Evaluated against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics and WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards.

## Limitations

- Qualitative scoring rather than a full quantitative measurement model
- Four-country sample — findings require broader empirical validation
- Bangladesh's National AI Policy remains in draft form (v1.1)
- Bias toward English-language and internationally indexed publications

---

## Future Work
